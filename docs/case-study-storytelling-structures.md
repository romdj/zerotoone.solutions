# Case Study Storytelling Structure Proposals

**Last Updated:** 2025-12-29

Three narrative approaches for redesigning case study pages to be more story-driven and engaging.

---

## Structure 1: The Problem-Journey-Transformation Arc

**Narrative Flow:** Classic story arc - conflict → struggle → resolution

### Sections:

#### 1. The Moment (Hook - 2-3 sentences)
Opens with a specific, visceral problem moment that anyone can understand.
- Example: *"Every day, millions of products moved through Nike's global supply chain. Every day, critical decisions were made blind. The data existed—but no one could see it."*

#### 2. The Challenge (Context)
What made this hard? Why hadn't it been solved before?
- Business constraints
- Technical complexity
- Organizational challenges
- Scale/scope

#### 3. The Insight (Turning Point)
What was the key realization that unlocked the solution?
- "We realized that..."
- "The breakthrough came when..."
- Shows strategic thinking, not just execution

#### 4. The Build (Journey)
How we approached it - as a narrative, not bullet points
- Key architectural decisions and WHY
- Challenges overcome
- Team collaboration
- Milestones hit

#### 5. The Transformation (Before/After)
Concrete outcomes told as stories
- "Before: X. After: Y."
- Specific examples of impact
- Measurable results in business context

#### 6. The Principles (What We Learned)
Broader insights extracted from this specific project
- Makes it thought-leadership, not just project report
- Demonstrates learning and adaptability

## Structure 2: The Stakeholder Perspectives Journey

**Narrative Flow:** Multiple viewpoints showing different angles of impact

### Sections:

#### 1. The Business Problem (Executive Perspective)
Opens from business stakeholder viewpoint
- Example: *"For Nike's supply chain executives, the question was simple: Where are our products? The answer was anything but."*

#### 2. The User Reality (End-User Perspective)
What did daily work look like before?
- Real scenarios from actual users
- Pain points in human terms
- Manual workarounds and frustrations

#### 3. The Technical Landscape (Engineering Perspective)
Why was the existing system failing?
- Legacy architecture constraints
- Integration nightmares
- Technical debt accumulation

#### 4. The Collaborative Solution (Our Approach)
How we brought these perspectives together
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Balancing competing needs
- Design decisions that served everyone

#### 5. The Ripple Effect (Impact Across Stakeholders)
How transformation affected each group differently
- Executives: strategic visibility
- Users: simplified workflows
- Engineers: maintainable system
- Business: measurable ROI

#### 6. The Lasting Change (Cultural Shift)
Beyond the technical - how thinking changed
- New ways of working
- Organizational learning
- Foundation for future innovation

## Structure 3: The Five Acts Dramatic Structure

**Narrative Flow:** Classical dramatic structure (Exposition → Rising Action → Climax → Falling Action → Resolution)

### Sections:

#### Act I: The World Before (Exposition)
Setting the stage - what was the status quo?
- The organization, the context, the existing approach
- Why it had worked in the past
- Why it couldn't work anymore
- Example: *"Nike's supply chain ran on systems built for a simpler era..."*

#### Act II: The Growing Crisis (Rising Action)
Escalating challenges forcing change
- Specific incidents or trends
- Missed opportunities becoming pattern
- Pressure mounting from multiple directions
- The decision to transform

#### Act III: The Breakthrough (Climax)
The key moment of architectural/strategic insight
- "Here's what we decided to do differently"
- The bold choice that defined the approach
- Why this was risky/ambitious
- Example: *"We proposed rebuilding from scratch with event-driven architecture - processing millions of events we weren't even capturing yet."*

#### Act IV: The Build (Falling Action)
Executing on the vision - challenges to resolution
- Building the platform
- Overcoming obstacles
- Team evolution
- Integration successes

#### Act V: The New Reality (Resolution)
What the world looks like now
- Concrete transformations
- New capabilities unlocked
- Future possibilities enabled
- Reflection on the journey

## Comparison Matrix

| Aspect | Structure 1: Arc | Structure 2: Perspectives | Structure 3: Five Acts |
|--------|------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| **Best For** | Technical readers who want strategic insight | Diverse audiences (exec to engineer) | Creating dramatic narrative tension |
| **Tone** | Analytical journey | Empathetic, multi-faceted | Epic, transformational |
| **Complexity** | Medium | High (multiple viewpoints) | Medium-High |
| **Emotional Impact** | Moderate | High (human stories) | Very High (dramatic arc) |
| **Length** | Shorter (~1000 words) | Longer (~1500 words) | Medium-Long (~1200 words) |
| **Focus** | Solution thinking | Stakeholder impact | Transformation narrative |

## Examples with Nike Supply Chain

### Structure 1 Example Opening:

> **The Moment**
>
> Every day, millions of products moved through Nike's global supply chain. Every day, critical decisions were made blind. The data existed—but no one could see it.
>
> **The Challenge**
>
> Nike's supply chain wasn't just big—it was Byzantine. Manufacturing across continents. Logistics partners with their own systems. SAP handling orders. Blue Yonder managing warehouses. Centiro coordinating transport. Each system knew its piece. No system knew the whole.

### Structure 2 Example Opening:

> **The Business Problem**
>
> "Where are our products?" For Nike's supply chain executives, this simple question had no simple answer. Products in transit? Check three systems. Warehouse status? Another two. Order fulfillment? Hope SAP and reality align.
>
> **The User Reality**
>
> For supply chain analysts, Monday mornings meant reconciliation hell. Export data from SAP. Pull logistics updates from Centiro. Cross-reference Blue Yonder. Build spreadsheets. By the time they had answers, the questions had changed.

### Structure 3 Example Opening:

> **Act I: The World Before**
>
> Nike's supply chain ran on systems built for a simpler era. When "global" meant a few countries. When "real-time" meant end-of-day batch reports. When the web of partners—SAP for orders, Blue Yonder for warehouses, Centiro for transport—could still be managed through scheduled syncs and manual reconciliation.
>
> Those days were over. But the systems remained.

## Recommended Approach

**For Elia (Enterprise Architecture):** Structure 2 (Stakeholder Perspectives)
- Multiple initiatives (Customer Centricity, ADEMAR, BSPM)
- Shows impact across organization
- Emphasizes cross-functional leadership

**For Nike (Supply Chain Platform):** Structure 3 (Five Acts)
- Dramatic transformation story
- Technical ambition deserves epic framing
- Global scale and high stakes

**For Philips (Healthcare Platform):** Structure 1 (Problem-Journey-Transformation)
- Clear problem → solution arc
- Technical depth with strategic clarity
- Healthcare compliance adds built-in stakes

## Implementation Notes

### Keep From Current Structure:
- Technical details (tools, technologies)
- Metrics and outcomes
- Architecture diagrams/patterns
- Team size and collaboration details

### Change:
- **Lead with story, not facts**
- **Use specific examples** instead of generalizations
- **Show tension and resolution** instead of just describing work
- **Make the reader feel the stakes** before revealing the solution

### Add:
- **Direct quotes** (even if paraphrased): "As the VP told us..."
- **Specific incidents**: "On launch day..." "Three months in..."
- **Before/after contrasts**: Vivid examples of transformation
- **Reflective insights**: What we learned that applies beyond this project

## Structure 4: The Technical Deep-Dive Journey

**Narrative Flow:** For technical audiences who want architectural thinking, not just outcomes

### Sections:

#### 1. The Architectural Challenge
What made this technically interesting/complex?
- Not just business context, but technical constraints
- Specific architectural problems to solve

#### 2. The Design Space
What options did we consider?
- Trade-offs evaluated
- Technologies compared
- Patterns debated

#### 3. The Key Decision
The architectural bet we made
- Why we chose this path
- What we gave up
- What we enabled

#### 4. Implementation Reality
How theory met practice
- Challenges during build
- Adaptations required
- Technical debt managed

#### 5. The System in Production
How it actually performs
- Real metrics (latency, throughput, cost)
- Lessons from operations
- Future evolution

**Narrative Flow:** Focuses on people and organizational transformation

### Sections:

#### 1. The Team We Inherited

- Process maturity
- Cultural challenges

#### 2. The Capability Gap
What needed to change
- Technical skills needed
- Organizational patterns required
- Mindset shifts necessary

#### 3. Building While Flying
How we developed the team while delivering
- Pairing and mentorship
- Progressive complexity
- Celebrating wins

#### 4. The Transformation
How the team evolved
- New capabilities acquired
- Confidence built
- Autonomy achieved

#### 5. The Legacy
What remained after we left

- Established practices
- Culture of excellence

## Structure 6: The Migration Chronicle

**Narrative Flow:** Perfect for legacy modernization stories

### Sections:

#### 1. The Legacy System
What we were replacing and why
- How it was built (and when)
- Why it served its time
- Why it couldn't continue

#### 2. The Migration Dilemma
Can't stop the business to rebuild
- Zero downtime requirement
- Data migration complexity
- User training challenge

#### 3. The Strangler Pattern
How we incrementally transformed
- Feature-by-feature migration
- Parallel run period
- Gradual cutover

#### 4. The Critical Moments
The scary parts of going live
- High-risk transitions
- Rollback plans
- Incident responses

#### 5. The New Foundation
What the modern system enables
- Capabilities impossible before
- Speed improvements
- Innovation unlocked

## Structure 7: The Metrics-Driven Narrative

**Narrative Flow:** Let the numbers tell the story

### Sections:

#### 1. The Baseline
Quantify the problem state
- Specific metrics before
- Business impact measured
- Cost of status quo

#### 2. The Target
What success looks like in numbers
- North star metrics
- Leading indicators
- Acceptable trade-offs

#### 3. The Inflection Points
Moments when metrics shifted
- First deployment: +X%
- Key optimization: +Y%
- Scale milestone: +Z%

#### 4. The Unexpected Outcomes
Metrics we didn't predict
- Positive surprises
- Secondary benefits
- New opportunities discovered

#### 5. The Current State
Where metrics are today
- Sustained improvements
- Ongoing optimization
- Future potential

## Structure 8: The Crisis → Resolution Arc

**Narrative Flow:** Opens with urgent problem, builds tension

### Sections:

#### 1. The Breaking Point
System/process at critical failure
- "The day everything crashed..."
- Specific incident that forced change
- Stakes made visceral

#### 2. The Damage Assessment
Understanding scope of problem
- Impact across organization
- Root cause analysis
- Systemic vs. symptomatic

#### 3. The Emergency Response
Immediate stabilization
- Quick fixes to stop bleeding
- Temporary workarounds
- Buying time for real solution

#### 4. The Rebuild
Addressing root causes
- Architectural re-thinking
- Process changes
- Organizational shifts

#### 5. The New Normal
How crisis led to transformation
- Stronger than before
- Preventive measures in place
- Culture change lasting

- Performance issues
- Process bottlenecks
- Team growth
