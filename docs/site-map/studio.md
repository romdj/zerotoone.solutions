### 2. The Journey (Consulting → Ventures Transition)

**Opening:**
"I started as a solution architect at Nike, IBM, and Philips—building systems that scale to millions of users. For years, I helped enterprises transform their platforms, modernize legacy systems, and architect cloud solutions.

But I kept thinking: 'What if I built my own companies?'

So I started building. First aerowheelcase (carbon fiber wheel cases for cyclists). Then tempsdarret.studio (photography). Then sports equipment R&D. Each venture taught me something consulting couldn't.

**Today:** Consulting provides the revenue and teaches me how to scale. Ventures provide the learning and prove what works.

**Tomorrow:** A full venture studio that applies enterprise-grade architecture to new companies—because I've done both.

This isn't about abandoning consulting. It's about expanding what 'building' means."

**Ethos:** Honest about where you are (consulting pays bills) and where you're going (venture studio)

**Success Metric:** > 70% of readers say "I understand the transition" (user testing)

### 4. Multidisciplinary Approach (Philosophy)

**Why We Build Across Domains**

**Photography (tempsdarret.studio)**
- Teaches creative problem-solving and aesthetic discipline
- Composition, lighting, storytelling—these inform how we design systems
- Visual thinking applies to architecture diagrams and user flows

**Engineering Research**
- 3D CAD, composite materials, aerodynamics
- Building physical products teaches precision and manufacturing constraints
- Software engineers often miss the physical-digital connection

**Athletics (Triathlon, Skiing, Cycling)**
- Discipline and endurance
- Long-term projects require the same stamina
- Building ventures is a marathon, not a sprint

**Design Thinking**
- Not just technical—we solve business problems
- Combining human-centered design with technical execution
- Solutions people actually use, not just technically perfect

**The Connection:**
"The best solutions come from cross-pollination. Enterprise architecture teaches us how to scale ventures. Building products teaches us how to ship fast for enterprises. Photography teaches us creative problem-solving. Athletics teaches us discipline.

It's all connected. It's all building."

**Ethos:** Not "I have random hobbies"—"These disciplines inform each other"

**Success Metric:** > 60% say "interesting person, makes sense" (vs "confused by random interests")

## Common Questions & Answers

### Q: "Is this an about page?"
**A:** Yes, but more. It's about + incubator + vision. "Studio" signals creative space, not just bio.

### Q: "Why 'Studio' instead of 'About'?"
**A:** "About" feels static (who we are). "Studio" feels active (where we create). Aligns with builder identity.

### Q: "Should incubator have its own page?"
**A:** No—it's part of the Studio identity. Separating it would fragment the story. Anchor link (`#incubator`) allows direct access.

### Q: "What if someone thinks multidisciplinary stuff is fluff?"
**A:** Enterprise buyers who don't care can skip it. Startup founders and venture partners value it. Page serves multiple audiences—not everyone reads everything.
